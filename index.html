Section 1.3 check

Check before you use

An AI tool can write a notice, a summary or a report in seconds. It can also get a date, a number or a name wrong and still sound completely sure. This check is about the habit that protects you: look closely before you send.

How is this check scored?
Questions about real decisions count for 2 marks. The few questions about remembering a term count for 1 mark. You need 70 percent of the total marks to pass. The result does not count towards your course duration.
Is there a special rule?
Yes. The first three stations (Confidence, Mark and Separate) are checked on their own as well. You need at least 70 percent in that group too. A high score in the later stations cannot make up for a low score here.
What happens if I get one wrong?
You see a short explanation straight away, plus a quick reminder of the part of Section 1.3 that covers it. You can retry the whole check as many times as you like.

Sounding sure is not the same as being right

AI tools are built to write smooth, confident text. They are not built to know whether each detail is true for your batch, your campus or your workshop.

Before you type, and before you trust

Never enter these into any AI tool. Not even to "check" something. Once typed, you cannot take it back.

Where to check instead

Go back to the place the information really comes from. Asking the same AI again, or a different AI, is not a check.

Warning signs in an AI reply

Exact numbers or percentages you never gave it.

Phrases like "as per the rules", "studies show" or "experts say" with no document named.

A person's name, a designation or an approval you did not mention.

A day and date that do not match each other on the calendar.

Details that sound helpful but are not in your source, such as "tools will be provided".
